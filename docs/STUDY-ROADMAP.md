# Readiness-based IELTS study roadmap

> **Status:** DRAFT — baseline results and external calibration may change the allocation

## Principle

The product supports the study plan; building or playing the game must not consume the time reserved for direct IELTS practice. The game receives 30–45 minutes of the learner's 150-minute daily budget.

There is no fixed exam deadline. Progression is controlled by evidence: baseline results, recurring-error reduction, delayed retention, unseen transfer, and comparable external assessments. Time spent building the product must still be limited so that software work does not displace direct IELTS practice.

An exam date is optional during normal study. After the learner chooses to book a test, the roadmap adds a time-bounded Exam Readiness phase calculated backward from that real date.

## Default daily allocation

| Activity | Minutes |
|---|---:|
| Atlas English foundation game | 35 |
| Direct timed or format-specific IELTS practice | 80 |
| Writing/Speaking production and review | 25 |
| Error log and next-day planning | 10 |
| **Total** | **150** |

One lighter or recovery day per week is allowed. Weekly allocation is adjusted from external calibration evidence, not only from game statistics.

## Initial skill weighting

Until the baseline is validated, direct IELTS time should emphasize Listening and Writing, then Speaking, while maintaining Reading:

| Skill | Initial share of direct IELTS time |
|---|---:|
| Listening | 30% |
| Writing | 30% |
| Speaking | 22% |
| Reading | 18% |

This weighting is provisional. Each four-week calibration review may change it.

## Phases

### Phase 1 — Validate and stabilize

Exit when the baseline is recorded, the main error families are mapped, and the weekly routine is sustainable:

- complete the baseline protocol in [`IELTS-ASSESSMENT-FRAMEWORK.md`](IELTS-ASSESSMENT-FRAMEWORK.md);
- identify the highest-frequency error families;
- build a sustainable daily routine;
- prioritize P0 grammar, spelling, pronunciation, and vocabulary nodes;
- avoid interpreting early mastery as band improvement.

### Phase 2 — Build foundations and question control

Exit when priority foundations show delayed retention and the learner can control the main question types at the agreed accuracy threshold:

- improve accuracy and delayed retention;
- train Listening/Reading question types and distractors;
- develop Writing paragraph control and Task 1 overview skills;
- develop Speaking extension, chunking, and fluency routines;
- run one four-week calibration cycle.

### Phase 3 — Increase transfer and timed work

Exit when gains in the game are supported by an improving trend on unseen, timed, externally calibrated work:

- reduce the share of isolated recognition tasks;
- increase production and IELTS Performance Layer tasks;
- practice under tighter time limits;
- review whether high mastery predicts unseen-task performance;
- run a broader mock or reviewed skill set.

### Phase 4 — Consolidate toward target readiness

Continue until comparable external evidence approaches the target profile with acceptable stability:

- increase complete sections, full Writing tasks, and complete Speaking practice;
- use the game mainly for targeted review and recurring errors;
- keep correcting the recognition-to-production gap;
- repeat comparable calibration rather than reacting to one unusually strong or weak result;
- recommend booking only when the approved readiness thresholds are met.

### Optional Phase 5 — Booked-exam readiness

Activated only after the learner has booked a real test date:

- prioritize complete sections and exam-condition work;
- use the game mainly for targeted review and recurring errors;
- avoid large curriculum expansions;
- rehearse pacing, recovery after mistakes, and test-day routine;
- taper workload in the final days rather than cramming new content.
- freeze large game features and content-system changes unless they fix a critical learning or reliability problem.

Not having a booked date is not a blocker for Phases 1–4, product planning, or learning validation.

## Product delivery boundary

The product first validates a complete deterministic online learning slice, then expands to the full MVP and cross-device account continuity. The study schedule must not wait for product development: full Writing, complete Speaking practice, pronunciation review by a person when needed, and timed sections continue through approved external materials.

## Weekly review

At the end of each week, record:

- completed study time by skill;
- external practice completed;
- recurring and corrected errors;
- overdue reviews;
- recognition-to-production gap;
- one priority to increase and one to reduce next week.

The study plan is successful only if external IELTS-like performance improves without unsustainable workload.
